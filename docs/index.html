A listing sits past its expected time on market and everyone in the room assumes the answer is price. Often it isn't. A reduction against the wrong constraint costs the seller real money and still doesn't sell the house.

This is a folder you drop into a Claude Project. It reconstructs the buyer funnel, compares it against your comps over the same weeks, finds where the listing actually breaks, and names one cause.

The default answer is a treatment, not a diagnosis

"Lower the price" gets prescribed for every stalled listing because it's the one lever an agent controls without spending money, and because it works often enough to stay believable.

But price is not one cause. It acts at three different points in the funnel and leaves a different fingerprint at each one.

In search results, price fails through thresholds, not affordability. A house at $512,000 is invisible to every search capped at $500,000 regardless of what it's worth. Before a showing, it fails through comparison against what else that money buys. After a showing, it fails as a value judgment by someone who already stood in the house.

Only the third one is fixed by a cut. The other two look identical from the outside.

A price cut that produced no change in activity has already falsified price as the constraint. The usual reading is that the cut was too small.

One rule does most of the work

Every listing moves buyers through four stages. Find the earliest one that falls below your comparable baseline. That stage is the diagnosis. Everything after it is starved and tells you nothing.

Savesat baseline

2nd showingsclearly below ← break
Offersstarved · not evidence

A listing pulling a third of normal views will also get few showings and no offers. Those are consequences, not separate faults. Reading them as independent problems is how you end up holding a list of nine issues and no idea which one is holding the sale.

When the break lands at views, nothing is concluded about the photos, the condition, or the walkthrough. Almost nobody reached them. When it lands at offers, the listing worked and the visit didn't, and a price cut is at its weakest, because those buyers already saw the house.

What comes back

Excerpt from a worked case. Fixed headings, every time.

Diagnosis · 4/2 single family · 58 days on market

Break point

Stage 4. The listing performed at or above baseline through the showing appointment. Buyers found it, clicked it, and came to the house at a normal rate. One of 34 first showings produced a second visit, against a comp baseline of roughly a quarter.

Primary cause

Expectation mismatch between the photographed property and the property buyers stood in. Seven of nine feedback notes name a discrepancy rather than a defect, and six of those name the same one: the main living space is materially smaller in person than the gallery suggests.

Alternatives, and why they are demoted

Price. Downstream and falsified. The day-34 reduction produced no change in showing rate over the following four weeks. The market was offered a lower number and did not respond.

Null model

Rejected. Three comps listed in the back half of the window sold in 14, 19, and 22 days.

Confidence

Supported

What would prove this wrong

A second-showing rate that is low across the entire comparison set, which would make the subject's figure normal rather than anomalous. That comp figure should be verified rather than assumed.

Three answers people don't expect

"Your listing is fine."

If the property sits inside the comp range at every funnel stage, there is no listing-specific problem and the segment slowed. This comes back more often than agents expect, and it's the finding that saves the most money. A reduction against a market-wide slowdown buys nothing except a lower sale price.

"Your reduction already answered this."

You ran the experiment. If activity didn't move, price was not the constraint, and the result is clean. Most people read an unresponsive cut as evidence the cut was too small.

"I can't tell you from this."

If the funnel can't be reconstructed from what you supplied, it names the missing input and stops rather than producing a confident cause built on nothing. That's intended behavior.

What it refuses to do

  • Recommend a price, a reduction, or a percentage
  • Rewrite your description or suggest headlines
  • Value the property. It is not a CMA or an appraisal
  • Hand you a ranked list of things to improve
  • Tell you what to do next

It stops at the cause, the evidence for it, and what would prove it wrong. A doctor doesn't hand you a rewritten body.

Install

  1. Create a Claude Project.
  2. Upload the contents of listing-stall-diagnostician/ to the project knowledge.
  3. Paste this into the project's custom instructions:
Follow identity.md and rules.md exactly. Run the diagnostic
sequence in rules.md in order and use the output contract at
the end of that file. Consult reference/ as needed.
Do not recommend actions.

No dependencies, no API keys, nothing to run.

Then feed it a case

Four things, or you get an Undetermined result rather than a wrong one: the listing with all photos, the price and status history, four or more comparables from the same submarket and window, and at least one funnel number (views, saves, inquiries, or showings).

That last one is the item most often skipped and the one the whole method depends on. It's what locates where the listing is failing. Without it every possible cause stays live.

Paste showing feedback verbatim. Don't summarize it. The pattern across notes is the evidence, and summarizing destroys it.

Open the folder on GitHub

Diagnoses listing performance against a comparison set you supply. Not an appraisal, a CMA, or a valuation, and it does not model your local market beyond the comps you give it.

There are deliberately no industry-average benchmarks anywhere in the folder. Showing rates and normal time on market vary too much by market, band, and season for a national figure to be anything but wrong and believed. Every baseline is computed from your own comps.

Worked examples in the repo are constructed teaching cases, not client files.
