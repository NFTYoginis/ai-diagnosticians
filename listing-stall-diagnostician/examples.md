# Examples

Three worked cases. They show the reasoning, not just the conclusion.

The cases are constructed for teaching, with figures chosen to make each discriminator
legible. They are not client files. The third one produces no defect, which is the
outcome most audit tools cannot reach and the one that saves the most money.

---

## Case 1 — Break at Stage 4

**Input:** 4/2 single family, 58 days on market, one prior reduction at day 34.
Comparison set of eight in the same school zone, overlapping band, same twelve weeks.
Portal views, showing log, nine verbatim feedback notes.

### Failure observed

Listing active 58 days. Comparison set DOM ranges 11 to 41 days, with six of eight
closed. Subject is beyond the top of the range. Failure confirmed.

### Comparison set

Eight properties, same school zone, $40k overlapping band, all listed within the subject's
window. Five sold, one pending, two active. Adequate for a range.

### Funnel reconstruction

| Stage | Subject (per week) | Comp range (per week) | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Views | 312 | 240 to 380 | At baseline |
| Saves | 19 | 12 to 26 | At baseline |
| Showings | 4.1 | 2.8 to 5.0 | At baseline |
| Second showings | 1 of 34 total | 6 to 11 per 30 first showings | **Clearly below** |
| Offers | 0 | 1 to 3 | Below, downstream |

### Break point

Stage 4. The listing performed at or above baseline through the showing appointment.
Buyers found it, clicked it, and came to the house at a normal rate. One of 34 first
showings produced a second visit against a comp baseline of roughly a quarter.

Stages 1 through 3 are not the diagnosis site and no conclusion is drawn about search
placement, price band positioning, or the photo set's ability to attract clicks. Those
mechanisms demonstrably worked.

### Primary cause

Expectation mismatch between the photographed property and the property buyers stood in.
Seven of nine feedback notes name a discrepancy rather than a defect, and six of those
seven name the same one: the main living space is materially smaller in person than the
gallery suggests. The listing is converting attention into visits and then losing every
buyer at the moment the promise meets the room.

### Evidence for this cause and against the alternatives

The second-showing rate is the discriminating figure. Buyers who like a house and want
to negotiate come back. A rate near zero against a comp baseline means buyers are not
leaving undecided, they are leaving resolved.

The feedback clusters on surprise rather than on a property attribute. "Smaller than it
looked" and "photos are misleading about the great room" describe a gap between two
impressions. A buyer objecting to a small living room would say the room is small. These
buyers are saying they expected something else, which locates the fault in the listing's
representation rather than in the house.

The gallery supports it. The three main-room images are shot from the corner at wide
angle with the sofa pushed to the walls, a combination that reads as roughly a third
larger than the stated square footage implies.

### Alternatives, and why they are demoted

**Price.** Downstream and falsified. The day-34 reduction produced no change in showing
rate over the following four weeks, which is a completed experiment: the market was
offered a lower number and did not respond. Two feedback notes mention price, both
paired with the size comment, which reads as a value judgment produced by the surprise
rather than an independent objection.

**Photo quality.** Not supported as a cause. The photos are performing well at the stage
photos are supposed to perform at. Views and showing conversion are at baseline. The
issue is what they represent, not how good they are.

**Condition.** Not supported. No feedback names condition, systems, or maintenance.

### Null model

Rejected. Comparison set DOM did not rise across the window: three comps listed in the
back half sold in 14, 19, and 22 days. The subject's Stage 1 through 3 figures track the
comp baseline, which is itself evidence against a segment-wide slowdown, since a frozen
market suppresses views and showings first.

### Confidence

**Supported.** The break is located by two independent measures and the feedback cluster
separates the live branches.

### Missing evidence

Open house attendance was not supplied. Floor plan absent, and its absence may itself be
contributing at Stage 4 since four comps carry one.

### What would prove this wrong

Feedback showing buyers had already seen the floor plan and dimensions before visiting
would weaken the mismatch reading substantially. So would a second-showing rate that is
low across the entire comparison set, which would make the subject's figure normal rather
than anomalous, and that comp figure should be verified rather than assumed.

## Case 2 — Break at Stage 1

**Input:** 3/2 townhouse, 47 days, no reductions. Comparison set of six. Portal views by
channel.

### Failure observed

47 days against a comp range of 9 to 28. Beyond the range. Confirmed.

### Funnel reconstruction

| Stage | Subject (per week) | Comp range (per week) | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Views | 61 | 190 to 340 | **Clearly below** |
| Saves | 3 | 9 to 21 | Below, downstream |
| Showings | 0.6 | 2.1 to 4.4 | Below, downstream |

### Break point

Stage 1. Views are under a third of the lowest comp. Every subsequent stage is starved.

Nothing is concluded about the photographs, the description, the condition, or the
walkthrough. No meaningful number of buyers reached any of them.

### Primary cause

Search threshold exclusion. The listing is priced at $412,000. Five of six comps sit
between $379,000 and $398,000, and the local convention in this band puts buyer filter
ceilings at $400,000 and $425,000. At $412,000 the listing is absent from every search
capped at $400,000, which is where the volume in this submarket sits, while competing in
the $425,000 ceiling searches against larger units.

### Evidence for this cause and against the alternatives

The view deficit is too large for a presentation cause. A weak hero image depresses
click-through on impressions that still occur. It does not remove a listing from the
results grid. A drop to under a third of the lowest comp indicates the listing is not
being served, not that it is being served and skipped.

Views are proportionally low across all three portals, which separates this from a
syndication gap. A feed problem produces uneven distribution.

Property attributes were verified against the tax record and the photos and match, which
rules out data-error exclusion.

### Alternatives, and why they are demoted

**Hero image.** Not supported at this magnitude, as above. Untested and remains live as a
secondary factor once visibility is resolved.

**Syndication.** Ruled out by even distribution across channels.

**Listing data error.** Ruled out by attribute verification.

**Description and search terms.** Not supported. The copy names the features the fast
comps name.

### Null model

Rejected. Comps moved at 9 to 28 days throughout the window, including two listed in the
final three weeks. The segment is not slow. The subject is uniquely invisible in it.

### Confidence

**Supported.**

### Missing evidence

Impression counts as distinct from view counts were not available on two portals. Those
would confirm the mechanism directly rather than by inference from the magnitude of the
view deficit.

### What would prove this wrong

Impression data showing the listing is being served at comp-normal rates and simply not
clicked. That would move the diagnosis to the hero image and invert the conclusion.

## Case 3 — Null

**Input:** 4/3 single family, 71 days. Agent requesting support for a second reduction.
Comparison set of nine. Full funnel data.

### Failure observed

71 days on market. The seller expected 30, based on a sale in the same neighborhood
fourteen months ago.

### Comparison set

Nine properties, same submarket and band, listed within the window. Two sold, three
pending, four active. DOM range 44 to 88 days, median 63.

### Funnel reconstruction

| Stage | Subject (per week) | Comp range (per week) | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Views | 204 | 155 to 260 | At baseline |
| Saves | 11 | 7 to 16 | At baseline |
| Showings | 2.4 | 1.6 to 3.1 | At baseline |
| Second showings | 7 of 24 | 5 to 9 per 24 | At baseline |
| Offers | 0 | 0 to 1 | At baseline |

### Break point

None located. The subject sits inside the comparison range at every stage of the funnel.

### Primary cause

**No listing failure is demonstrated.** The subject's 71 days sits inside a comp range of
44 to 88, eight days above the median. Every funnel stage tracks the comparison set. Six
of nine comps are still active or pending after comparable time.

The segment slowed. Comps from the first four weeks of the window carry DOM of 44 to 58.
Comps listed after week six carry 67 to 88. The subject is performing normally inside a
market that has changed.

### Evidence for this cause and against the alternatives

A listing with a real constraint fails at an identifiable stage. This one does not. It
attracts views at baseline, converts them to showings at baseline, and produces second
showings at baseline, which means buyers are finding it, visiting it, and returning to it
at the rate the market is currently producing.

The seller's 30-day expectation comes from a transaction fourteen months old, outside the
comparison window, in a different rate environment. It is not a baseline.

### Alternatives, and why they are demoted

**Price.** Not supported. The subject converts views to showings and showings to second
visits at comp-normal rates. Buyers are not filtering it out on price or rejecting it on
value after visiting. The prior reduction at day 41 produced a small view bump consistent
with the general refresh effect and no change in showing conversion.

**Presentation and condition.** Not supported. Both would appear as a below-range stage
and neither does.

### Null model

**Accepted.** This is the diagnosis. The comparison set's DOM rose across the window, the
subject tracks the set at every stage, and six of nine comps remain unsold at comparable
time.

### Confidence

**Supported.** Nine comps with full funnel data and a clear within-window trend.

### Missing evidence

Band-level absorption and inventory figures were not supplied. They would confirm the
segment reading directly rather than by inference from the comp set's own DOM drift.

### What would prove this wrong

Absorption data showing the band is clearing at prior speed, which would mean the comp
set is unrepresentative and the subject's normality is an artifact of a badly chosen
comparison group.

### Note on what this case is for

The requested output was support for a second reduction. The evidence does not support
one. A reduction here does not address a constraint, because no constraint specific to
this property was found. What it does is lower the price the property eventually sells
at in a market where comparable homes are also waiting.

Naming that is the diagnosis. What to do about a slow market is the agent's conversation
with their seller, and this folder does not have an opinion about it.
